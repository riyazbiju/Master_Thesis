\section{Thesis Outline}\label{sec:intro_outline}

The remainder of this thesis is structured as follows.
Chapter~\ref{ch:fundamentals} introduces BMW's quality process and the role
of causer attribution within it, followed by the foundations of knowledge
graphs, link prediction, knowledge graph embeddings, graph neural networks
and explainable AI.
Chapter~\ref{ch:related_work} reviews related work on decision support for
quality-incident handling, link prediction on industrial knowledge graphs,
the comparison of link prediction model families, semi-inductive and
inductive link prediction, and the explanation of link predictions and its
evaluation, and derives the research gap.
Chapter~\ref{ch:problem} formalizes first-call causer attribution as
semi-inductive link prediction, defines the information available at
prediction time, the label derivation and the novelty levels, and describes
the data sources and graph schema.
Chapter~\ref{ch:method} presents the three model families and how each one
produces an explanation.
Chapter~\ref{ch:setup} details the experimental setup, including the
temporal split, the leakage controls, the novelty-level evaluation, the
fidelity protocol and the metrics.
Chapter~\ref{ch:results} reports and discusses the results, including
examples of each model family's explanations, and the limitations of the
approach.
Chapter~\ref{ch:conclusion} answers the research questions and outlines
future work.
